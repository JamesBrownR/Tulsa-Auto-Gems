Timing Belt Replacement

Protect Your Engine from Catastrophic Failure

Why Timing Belt Replacement Matters

Your timing belt is one of the most critical components in your engine. It synchronizes the rotation of the crankshaft and camshaft, ensuring your engine's valves open and close at precisely the right times. When a timing belt fails, the results can be catastrophic.

The Cost of Failure

In interference engines (common in Honda, Acura, and many Toyota models), a broken timing belt causes pistons and valves to collide, resulting in:

  • Bent valves
  • Damaged pistons
  • Cylinder head damage
  • Potential complete engine failure
  • Repair costs of $3,000-$6,000 or more

When to Replace Your Timing Belt

By Mileage

Honda & Acura: Typically 60,000-100,000 miles

Toyota & Lexus: Generally 60,000-90,000 miles

Older models: May require replacement as early as 60,000 miles

Always consult your owner's manual for your specific vehicle's recommended interval.

By Age

Replace every 7-10 years regardless of mileage

Rubber deteriorates over time, even if the car isn't driven frequently

Oklahoma's temperature extremes can accelerate belt degradation

Warning Signs

  • Ticking noise from engine
  • Engine misfires
  • Rough idling
  • Oil leaks near timing belt cover
  • Engine won't start

Why Replace the Water Pump?

Most mechanics recommend replacing the water pump during timing belt service because:

  • It's located in the same area (labor is already accounted for)
  • Water pumps typically fail around the same mileage as timing belts
  • Replacing it prevents another expensive labor job later
  • Minimal additional cost compared to doing it separately
